Simon Lacey founded Market Access X to apply twenty-five years of trade policy experience to a question that platforms like S&P GTA and Maplecroft don't answer: how should firms reposition when regulatory pressure, export controls, and shifting industrial geography rearrange their operating environment?

Few analysts have seen this terrain from inside the firm being targeted. Lacey served as VP Global Government Affairs at Huawei Technologies from 2015 to 2019, leading the trade and investment policy function across more than a dozen markets through the 5G security crisis. The brief covered discriminatory market access measures, investment screening reviews, and procurement exclusions framed as security policy across Europe, India, Southeast Asia, and the Americas, as well as the firm's response to the trade and investment disruptions set in motion by Brexit. He then spent three years as Associate Professor in International Trade Law at the University of Adelaide, followed by two years leading Digital Trade and Geopolitics at the World Economic Forum in Geneva.

The earlier work was operational. Lacey directed a multi-regional trade policy unit at the World Trade Institute in Berne, was later embedded inside the Indonesian Ministry of Trade as senior counsel to the Minister, led a project at the World Bank and later a team at the Asian Development Bank, and has advised governments and firms across more than thirty countries in Europe, Central Asia, Southeast Asia, the Pacific, the Middle East, Africa, and the Americas. He holds an LLM in international economic law from Georgetown University Law Center and a Bachelor of Law from the University of Fribourg in Switzerland.

Coverage at Market Access X follows the chokepoints. Critical minerals and the new energy systems built on them (batteries, grid-scale storage, renewable generation), pharmaceuticals, and technology infrastructure (including semiconductors, AI, digital platforms, and IoT systems) are the three sectors where regulatory mechanics most directly determine which firms gain and lose ground over the next decade. The platform exists to make those mechanics legible to the people who have to act on them.

Analytical Approach

Three principles that shape every report and article published on Market Access X.

01

Follow the money, not the narrative

Understanding who benefits from a regulatory restriction is more analytically revealing than accepting the policy framing at face value. Trade controls always have constituencies — naming them is the first analytical move.

02

Companies aren't passive victims

Regulatory pressure creates strategic repositioning opportunities. The firms that move early — like Lynas navigating Malaysia license risk — capture disproportionate value. Analysis that treats firms as objects misses the point.

03

Chokepoints are self-defeating over time

Supply chain leverage degrades the moment it's deployed — triggering the substitution investments that erode it. The timeline is the strategic variable. Analysis that ignores the clock misreads the situation.
