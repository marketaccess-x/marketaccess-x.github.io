Market Access Sentry

Monitoring Trade Barriers to China's Exports in the Global South

China's export surge has shifted from the wealthier and more mature markets of the United States and Europe to countries in the so-called "Global South". Our Market Access Sentry tracks how policymakers in those markets are responding. For each market and sector it sets the barriers put in place against trade outcomes in the same products. A market counts as closing only when Chinese goods lose ground or are forced to price up, not when measures accumulate on paper.

How the status is set
Sources, method and caveats
Policy measures

Harmful import measures affecting China, implemented by the market concerned, from Global Trade Alert. Only border instruments count toward coverage: trade remedies, safeguards, tariffs, licensing and quotas, and import charges. Investment and local-content measures are shown separately, because they are built to bring Chinese production in rather than keep Chinese goods out. Each measure links to its record.

Which barriers count

Coverage counts measures put in place since January 2022 and still in force. Earlier measures are treated as part of the baseline. Phased tariff schedules are read as one continuing measure. Measures that fall on a sector's product lines but target a different product (for example, phone and wearables batteries) are excluded, as are low-value parcel and e-commerce rules, which fall on consumer parcels rather than bulk trade. A short list of further records, reviewed individually, is set aside with reasons recorded.

Coverage weights

Each product line is weighted by China's exports to that market in 2023, as recorded by the importing country. Fixed weights stop a barrier from shrinking its own coverage by cutting the trade it targets.

Trade outcomes

Monthly imports at the six-digit product level from UN Comtrade, summed over rolling twelve-month windows. China's share in each market is compared with its share in a fixed panel of comparison importers, re-weighted to the market's own product mix. Prices are China's unit values per kilogram into the market against into the panel, product by product; lines whose price moves more than threefold are treated as product-mix noise, and a wedge beyond ±40% is not used. The panel includes Mexico and Canada, both of which have raised barriers of their own. Results for India (EVs and batteries, aluminum) and Brazil (steel, solar) depend in part on that choice: with both removed, all four read contested rather than closing.

Vietnam

Vietnam's own import data stop in 2023. Its outcomes use China's reported exports to Vietnam against China's exports worldwide in the same products, annually. Its status is therefore less timely than the others.

Dates and lags

Measures are counted from their implementation date; open investigations appear separately. New measures reach the database with a lag, and trade data arrive two to four months after the month they describe, so the latest months are provisional.

What the data cannot show

Informal customs and port friction is not captured. A thin measure record for a market is not evidence that its door is open. Unit values move with product mix within a product line as well as with price.
