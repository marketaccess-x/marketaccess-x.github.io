⚙ The core analytical conclusion

Beijing transfers technology when Chinese capital controls the destination supply chain. Beijing blocks transfer when Western capital seeks to acquire Chinese-origin technology independent of Chinese ownership. The NDRC's April 27, 2026 order blocking Meta's acquisition of Manus AI is the public crystallization of a policy architecture years in the making, one that the IRA, CRMA, and proposed Industrial Accelerator Act are all premised on being able to circumvent. They cannot.

The analytical problem this report solves

Western industrial policy has proceeded on an assumption that Chinese manufacturing technology, process knowledge, and engineering talent can be accessed through commercial arrangements while Chinese firms are simultaneously excluded from the ownership and supply chain positions that make such transfer commercially rational for the Chinese side. Beijing has now formally invalidated that assumption.

This report maps the regulatory architecture Beijing has constructed since 2021 across three sectors, battery manufacturing, critical minerals processing, and artificial intelligence, and tests the thesis against evidence that complicates it. The Indonesian HPAL counterexample is examined in full: it does not contradict the thesis. It sharpens it. Chinese firms transfer technology when Chinese capital controls the destination. They block transfer when it exits the Chinese ecosystem. The IRA and CRMA demand the latter while progressively restricting the former.

Key findings

Finding 01

The regulatory architecture governing technology outflows has crossed a qualitative threshold since 2021, shifting from a trade-facilitation regime to a security-centric framework that treats manufacturing process knowledge as a core national security asset.

Finding 02

The Manus order is the public signal of a policy direction already embedded in export control catalogues, dual-use regulations, and foreign investment security review measures, not the origin point of a new doctrine.

Finding 03

Presence without transfer is the only model Beijing now consistently sanctions for Western partners: physical manufacturing footprint, yes; process knowledge, manufacturing execution system software, or core IP, no.

Finding 04

The July 2025 TIER revision placed spodumene-to-lithium carbonate, spodumene-to-lithium hydroxide, and direct lithium extraction technologies on the restricted export list, converting a commercial advantage into a regulatory instrument at the precise moment Western processors are failing to close the cost gap independently.

Finding 05

The Singapore incorporation strategy has not failed categorically. It has been selectively pierced for AI-sector acquisitions and frontier technology exits where Western capital seeks to acquire Chinese-origin IP independent of ongoing Chinese control.

Finding 06

US FEOC rules and Beijing's export control architecture are converging on the same commercial transactions from opposite directions. Any transaction viable under one regime must now be tested against the other. The Gotion Michigan termination is the template.
