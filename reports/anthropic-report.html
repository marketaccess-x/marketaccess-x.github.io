The analytical problem this report solves

In early March 2026, the U.S. Department of War formally designated Anthropic — an American AI company — as a supply chain risk to national security. Anthropic is the first domestic company ever to receive a designation created to exclude Huawei and Kaspersky from U.S. government systems. It happened because Anthropic refused to remove two contractual restrictions: no mass domestic surveillance of Americans, and no fully autonomous weapons without human oversight.

On 26 March 2026, a federal judge found the designation likely unlawful and granted a preliminary injunction. The litigation, the coalition that formed against the government, the commercial fallout, and the judicial response together constitute the most consequential AI-government confrontation in U.S. history. The outcome will define the terms on which every technology vendor engages with the federal government for the next decade.

Key findings

Finding 01

A statute designed for foreign adversaries is being applied to a domestic company. The use of “adversary” in the statutory definition is not semantic ambiguity — it is the core legal weakness in the government’s position, now confirmed by the court.

Finding 02

The designation is narrower than the political rhetoric suggests. Formal legal effect attaches primarily to DoD procurements with specific DFARS and FAR clauses — not the government-wide ban announced on social media. The injunction pauses enforcement of all three actions.

Finding 03

The non-obvious precedent risk is the secondary boycott. The attempt to prohibit contractors from all commercial use of Anthropic — not merely government-contract use — is the most legally novel and most dangerous element. Judge Lin found no statutory basis for it.

Finding 04

OpenAI’s Pentagon deal reportedly contains the same two restrictions that triggered Anthropic’s designation. The political inconsistency is the clearest evidence that the designation is driven by retaliation, not genuine security risk assessment.

Finding 05

Claude was reportedly used in active U.S. military operations hours after the ban was announced. A supply chain risk that the national security apparatus cannot operationally eliminate — and is actively using in combat — is not a supply chain risk under any coherent reading of the statute.

Finding 06

The nuclear safety dimension is the most underreported consequence. Forced removal of Claude from national laboratories terminates an active AI safety evaluation program in the highest-stakes domain that exists.

Finding 07

The preliminary injunction ruling is a comprehensive repudiation of the government’s legal position. Judge Lin called the application of the supply chain risk designation to Anthropic’s conduct “Orwellian” and found the administrative record was generated after the fact to justify a foreordained conclusion.

Finding 08

The pretext finding is analytically significant beyond this case. Judge Lin found that the government cannot cure the designation through amended procedures alone — because the First Amendment retaliation problem is independent of whether the process was clean.
