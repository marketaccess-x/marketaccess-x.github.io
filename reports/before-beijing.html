The analytical problem this report solves

The Geneva truce (May 2025), the Stockholm extension (August 2025), the Busan agreement (October 2025) — three chapters of the same negotiation, each buying time that neither side knew how to spend. The visit to Beijing that was meant to extend it has been delayed by an Iran war that neither side anticipated, but is now scheduled for mid-May.

The dominant policy narrative presents these as a sequence of diplomatic achievements. This report makes the case that they are a sequence of temporary suspensions built on a legal framework the US Supreme Court partially demolished in February 2026. Every concession is revocable. Every expiry date is approaching. The November cliff — when the Busan tariff suspension, the rare earth control pause, and the dual-use ban suspension all converge in a 17-day window — is the most consequential risk event on the bilateral calendar, and most organisations have not begun to model it.

Key findings

Finding 01

The Busan framework is one year, fragile, and structurally incomplete. The two sides' readouts diverge on scope. No joint communiqué was issued. The White House overstated China's rare earth concessions; MOFCOM's language was considerably more limited.

Finding 02

The Supreme Court's February 2026 IEEPA ruling voided the legal basis for the tariff architecture negotiated at Busan. The replacement Section 122 tariffs expire after 150 days. New Section 301 authorities will not be in place until late 2026 at earliest.

Finding 03

The upcoming summit's deliverables will be commercial, not structural. Agricultural purchases, Boeing orders, and energy trade are achievable. Tariff reductions, semiconductor controls, and TikTok are not on the table. A CSIS survey of 79 former US officials found zero who believed both sides would fully keep Busan commitments.

Finding 04

China's mineral export control architecture is permanent and expanding regardless of diplomatic temperature. The April 2025 heavy rare earth controls and February 2025 tungsten and tellurium controls were never suspended. Busan paused only the most recent escalatory additions.

Finding 05

The November 2026 cliff is the single most important risk event on the bilateral calendar. Busan tariff suspensions, REE control pauses, and the dual-use ban suspension all expire in a 17-day window. Sectors with no contingency plan face acute disruption with limited notice.

Finding 06

China has more leverage entering this summit than any previous bilateral engagement. The IEEPA ruling weakened the US tariff threat, the Iran war distracted Washington's preparation, and China's mineral whitelist system has evolved into a permanent architecture of state-managed commodity access.

⚠ Non-obvious risk

The April 2025 controls on seven heavy rare earth elements — terbium, dysprosium, yttrium, scandium, lutetium, samarium, and gadolinium — were never part of the Busan suspension and remain fully in force. Exports to US military end-users are completely blocked. An F-35 requires approximately 900 pounds of rare earth magnets. A Virginia-class submarine requires 9,200 pounds. Most defence procurement risk assessments are modelling a suspended control environment that does not exist for these materials.
