Neither Vassalage Nor Expropriation: Reading Australia's Northern Minerals Order

17 May 2026 Critical Minerals Rare Earths FIRB
Diversification is no longer about finding non-Chinese supply, which exists in adequate quantities for most critical minerals. It is about building a regulatory, financial, and commercial perimeter able to compete at scale.

On May 17, 2026, Australian Treasurer Jim Chalmers signed an order directing six China-linked entities to divest approximately 17.58% of Northern Minerals Limited. This was met with a fair amount of outrage in official and Chinese media channels, who, among other things, claim this is both a geopolitical move demonstrating yet again Australia's subservience to US interests, while also decrying this as effectively tantamount to a sudden and unprecedented act of expropriation. Neither of these claims stands up to closer scrutiny.

With regard to the first claim, it is generally accepted that China accounts for roughly 70 percent of global rare earth mining production (already a high number), whereas in the processing segment, that figure is closer to 90 percent, which is alarming and constitutes an unhealthy choke point. This fact has been the rationale behind multilateral efforts to diversify rare earth processing and reduce dependency on a single supplier. The concern is structural rather than country-specific. Countries that hold chokepoints and come under strategic pressure tend to use them. Import-dependent economies are also acting rationally in seeking to dilute that concentration. The chokepoint structure is the problem, not the identity of whoever currently holds it and chooses to wield it.

Addressing the second claim, the Australian government's efforts to manage Chinese ownership of critical mineral assets date back a number of years. It was Josh Frydenberg, Treasurer in the Morrison government, who in 2020 prohibited a placement by Baogang Group, an Inner Mongolia SASAC entity that is the controlling shareholder of China Northern Rare Earth. Chinese efforts then focused on acquiring a similar stake via a different, privately owned entity, namely Yuxiao Fund. These efforts were themselves resisted by the next and current Treasurer (Jim Chalmers), first by means of a February 2023 cap, then a June 2024 disposal order, then a June 2025 Federal Court action that was the first FATA litigation since 1975. These measures were in turn followed by Justice Perram's January 2026 A$14m penalty for efforts to evade the June 2024 disposal order through sham transfers to a related party. Following this, there was an April 2026 interim direction freezing voting and transfer rights over Hong Kong Ying Tak's approximately 4 percent stake while the FIRB investigated a second wave of related-party transfers from the original 2024 disposal-order targets. Then finally, and the most recent at the time of writing, we have the May 2026 order. Just in case there was any doubt that these interests were acting in a coordinated fashion, the Yuxiao Group's November 2023 strategic cooperation agreements with China Northern Rare Earth confirm the same Inner Mongolia network operates across both Frydenberg's 2020 denial order and the more recent chain of actions under Treasurer Chalmers.

Australian mining exports to China continue at a healthy clip, with iron ore alone running at A$116bn in FY24-25, alongside bauxite, copper concentrate, and gold, and the 51% Chinese-owned Tianqi-IGO Kwinana lithium hydroxide refinery continues to operate. The May 17 order targets one heavy rare earth conversion bottleneck and one shareholder network attempting capture of value-added Australian processing output. In essence, what this is really about is as follows: diversification is no longer about finding non-Chinese-controlled supply, which exists in adequate quantities for most critical minerals. Rather, it is about constructing a regulatory, financial, and commercial perimeter that is able to compete at scale with China's now impressive, globe-spanning and market-dominating capabilities in this sector. Northern Minerals is an operational test case, currently unfolding in real time, of whether this perimeter can actually be built.
